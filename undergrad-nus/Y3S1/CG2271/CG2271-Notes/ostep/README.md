# Preface

{% hint style="warning" %}
When I learned NUS CG2271, I chose this classic OS textbook called OSTEP as my main textbook!
{% endhint %}

The book is called **Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces**, and the title is obviously an homage to one of the greatest sets of lecture notes ever created, by one **Richard Feynman** on the topics of Physics. The three easy pieces refer to the three major thematic elements the book is organized around:

1. [Virtualization](introduction-to-os.md#virtualization)
2. [Concurrency](introduction-to-os.md#concurrency)
3. [Persistance](introduction-to-os.md#persistance)

At the beginning of each major section, we'll first present an **abstraction** that an operating system provides, and then work in subsequent chapters on the mechanisms, policies, and other support needed to provide the abstraction. Abstractions are fundamental to all aspects of Computer Science, so it is perhaps no surprise that they are also essential in operating systems.

{% hint style="success" %}
The idea of abstraction appears again in the field of OS! We've seen that in [DDCA](https://app.gitbook.com/s/jTJFBPtKk6NwweAooH53/textbook/from-zero-to-one#abstraction) and many other IC design fields!
{% endhint %}

In this book's preface, the authors mentioned a great point on the point of education which I'd like to note it down for future reference! Yeats famously said "Education is not the filling of a pail but the lighting of a fire." He was right but wrong at the same time. The real point of education is:

> to get you interested in something, to learn something more about the subject matter on your own and not just what you have to digest to get a good grade in some class.

{% hint style="success" %}
As one of the author's fathers used to say, "Learn beyond the classroom"!
{% endhint %}

We created these notes to spark your interest in operating systems, to read more about the topic on your own, to talk to your professor about all the exciting research that is going on in the field, and even to get involved with that research. It is a great field(!), full of exciting and wonderful ideas that have shaped computing history in profound and important ways. And while we understand this fire won't light for all of you, we hope it does for many, or even a few. Because once that fire is lit, well, that is when you truly become capable of doing something great. And thus the real point of the educational process:

> to go forth, to study many new and fascinating topics, to learn, to mature, and most importantly, to find something that lights a fire for you.

{% hint style="success" %}
Really amazing and warming words from the authors of OSTEP :sob:!
{% endhint %}
